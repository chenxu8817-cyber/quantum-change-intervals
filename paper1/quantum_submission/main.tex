\begin{abstract}
We consider exact minimum-error localization of a transient change in a
sequence of independent pure-state outputs. The source emits
\(\lvert0\rangle\), switches abruptly to a calibrated state
\(\lvert\psi\rangle\) on one nonempty contiguous interval, and then returns
perfectly to \(\lvert0\rangle\). The outputs may be stored coherently and
measured collectively. For a known interval length \(i\), the candidate-state
Gram matrix is
\(G_{ab}=r^{\min(\lvert a-b\rvert,i)}\), where
\(c=\lvert\langle0\vert\psi\rangle\rvert\) and \(r=c^2\). For each fixed \(i\), its
Toeplitz symbol gives an exact integral for the asymptotic Bayes success
probability, and the square-root measurement (SRM) attains the same limit.
When both the known length and the number of admissible translations diverge,
with no restriction on their ratio, the optimal success probability converges
to the one-dimensional Toeplitz (single-change) functional evaluated at the
effective compound overlap \(c^2\), namely \(p_1(c^2)\).
For an unknown length, the physical Gram kernel is not globally
two-dimensional Toeplitz because separated intervals carry a gap-dependent
correction. Under the prior uniform over all nonempty intervals, we remove a
vanishing-prior short sector, use a triangular Følner reduction, and transfer
the result back to both the Bayes optimum and the SRM of the full physical
family. An exceptional-sector Gram transfer theorem isolates the reusable
mechanism behind this step. The resulting, prior-specific limit is
\(p_1(c)^2\). For the stated conditional uniform anomaly prior, adding a
no-change hypothesis with fixed prior changes the optimal joint Bayes limit to
\(\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)L\), where \(L\) is the conditional localization limit; no
augmented weighted-SRM claim is made. Finite-size semidefinite programs and
full dense physical-Gram SRM computations illustrate the asymptotic results.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} quantum change point; quantum state
discrimination; change interval; square-root measurement; Toeplitz operator;
Følner sequence; Bayesian detection
