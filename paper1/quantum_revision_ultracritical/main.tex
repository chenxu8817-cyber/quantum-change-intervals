\begin{abstract}
We study exact-label minimum-error localization of a transient change in
independent quantum outputs. The source emits a known pure state
\(\lvert\psi\rangle\) on one nonempty contiguous interval and the reference
state \(\lvert0\rangle\) elsewhere, while the receiver may measure the full
sequence collectively. Write
\(c=\lvert\langle0\vert\psi\rangle\rvert\) and let \(p_1(x)\) denote the
one-dimensional Poisson-kernel Toeplitz functional. For fixed \(c\), the
corresponding Toeplitz symbol gives an exact square-root integral for every
fixed known interval length, and the square-root measurement (SRM) and the
Bayes optimum converge to the same limit. If the known length and the number
of translations both diverge, their common limit is \(p_1(c^2)\). Under the
prior uniform over all \(M_n=n(n+1)/2\) nonempty intervals, a triangular
F{\o}lner reduction and a transfer to the physical Gram matrix instead give
the common limit \(p_1(c)^2\).

We also resolve moving overlaps near the indistinguishable limit at \(c_n=1\),
where the exact-label baseline is \(1/M_n\). With
\(\tau_n=n(1-c_n)(\log n)^2\), uniformly for \(\tau_n\) in every fixed compact
interval, the trace benchmark, SRM, and optimum obey
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_nP_X&=
 \left(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\tau_n}}{\pi}
 +\frac{\sqrt2\,\tau_n}{\pi^2}\right)^2\\
 &\quad+O\!\left(\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\right),
 \end{aligned}
\]
for \(X\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{SRM},\mathrm{opt}\}\).
On this scale, \(n p_1(c_n)\) remains bounded. In the complementary regime
\[
 c_n\to1,\qquad c_n<1\ \text{eventually},\qquad
 n p_1(c_n)\to\infty,
\]
all three criteria satisfy \(P_X\sim p_1(c_n)^2\), without a monotonicity
assumption. This includes \(c_n=1-\lambda/n\), for which
\(M_nP_X/(\log n)^4\to2\lambda^2/\pi^4\) uniformly when \(\lambda\) ranges
over compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\). Finally, we add a no-change hypothesis
with fixed prior \(\pi_0\), retaining the stated uniform interval prior
conditional on an anomaly. If \(\Lambda\) denotes the corresponding
asymptotic conditional localization limit, then the joint Bayes detection and
exact localization limit is \(\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)\Lambda\). For fixed \(c<1\),
the prior-weighted SRM attains this
limit for growing known and unknown interval lengths. At \(c=1\), however,
the raw weighted SRM tends to \(\pi_0^2\), whereas the optimum tends to
\(\pi_0\); maximum-a-posteriori relabeling restores the optimal limit.
\end{abstract}
