\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

Quantum change point inference asks where an ordered source switches from one
state preparation process to another. A quantum receiver may store the emitted
systems and measure the entire sequence collectively rather than decide from
each output in turn. Early Bayesian work considered unknown pure states and
two candidate change locations \cite{akimoto2011}. For a permanent change
between known pure states, collective minimum-error discrimination has a
nonzero asymptotic success probability and can outperform online strategies
based on local measurements \cite{sentis2016}. Protocols for exact and online
unambiguous identification were developed later
\cite{sentis2017,sentis2018}, and an adaptive
Bayesian protocol was demonstrated with photonic state sequences
\cite{yu2018}. Nearby formulations address different operational questions. Quickest and
sequential quantum change detection balance false alarms against detection
delay \cite{fanizza2023,zecchin2026}. Hamiltonian change points have been
studied as finite quantum process discrimination problems under a
minimum-error criterion \cite{nakahira2023}, whereas unambiguous channel
discrimination permits an inconclusive outcome \cite{nakahira2026}. Other
work treats general Gram matrix semidefinite programs, multiple changes,
isolated anomalies, and boundaries between quantum domains
\cite{mohan2023,skotiniotis2024,llorens2024,llorens2025}.

In this work, the change is transient. The source emits a known anomalous pure
state \(\lvert\psi\rangle\) on one nonempty contiguous interval and then
returns to the reference state \(\lvert0\rangle\). We call this returning
structure a \emph{quantum change interval}. For a sequence of length
\(n\) and an interval \(I=[a,b]\), the corresponding hypothesis is
\[
\lvert\Phi_{a,b}\rangle
=\lvert0\rangle^{\otimes(a-1)}
 \lvert\psi\rangle^{\otimes(b-a+1)}
 \lvert0\rangle^{\otimes(n-b)}.
\]
We write \(i=b-a+1\) for the interval length,
\(c=\lvert\langle0\vert\psi\rangle\rvert\) for the local state overlap, and
\(r=c^2\). When \(i\) is known, there are \(N=n-i+1\) admissible translations
\(a=1,\dots,N\), so only the starting point \(a\) must be inferred.
When the length is
unknown, both endpoints must be inferred. Figure~\ref{fig:model-geometry}
shows the returning geometry and the collective localization task.

Success in this paper means exact recovery of the interval label:
\(\widehat I=I\). A minimum-error positive operator-valued measure (POVM)
reports an interval on every run and may be wrong. By contrast, unambiguous
exact identification allows an inconclusive outcome but requires every
conclusive report to be correct \cite{sentis2017,llorens2024}.
Certified answer discrimination instead limits the distance between the
reported and true ordered labels \cite{martinez2019}. We study exact-label
minimum-error localization and later add a hypothesis \(H_0\) of no change.

The unknown-length problem is not a standard two-dimensional Toeplitz problem.
For intersecting or adjacent intervals, the overlap depends only on
the endpoint displacement and agrees with a separable Toeplitz kernel. A
positive gap introduces an additional correction. The physical Gram matrix
is therefore neither a global two-dimensional Toeplitz compression nor an
element of a transitive association scheme, so a direct multilevel
Szeg{\H o} theorem does not give the desired limit.

\paragraph{Main results.}
Let \(p_1(x)\) be the one-dimensional Poisson kernel Toeplitz functional
defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:p1-definition}. Toeplitz spectral methods
\cite{gray2006,tilli1998,tyrtyshnikov1998}, together with the local Følner
argument developed below, yield the asymptotic laws below.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  For known length \(i\geq1\), under the uniform prior over
  \(N=n-i+1\) translations, the optimal and square-root measurement (SRM)
  success probabilities converge to the same integral of the residual
  one-dimensional Toeplitz symbol. The result holds for \(0\leq r\leq1\).
  For \(0<r<1\), their difference is \(O_{i,r}(N^{-1/2})\).
\item
  If \(i=i_n\to\infty\) and \(N_n=n-i_n+1\to\infty\), both probabilities
  converge to \(p_1(c^2)\). No condition on \(i_n/N_n\) is required.
\item
  For unknown interval length, under the uniform prior over all
  \(n(n+1)/2\) intervals, both probabilities converge to \(p_1(c)^2\). The
  exceptional-sector theorem used here applies to any equiprobable pure-state
  ensemble for which the retained mass tends to one, the retained physical
  Gram matrix converges in operator norm to a comparison matrix, and the
  comparison square-root diagonal has the stated local limit. Under these
  conditions, it transfers both the optimal and SRM limits to the full
  ensemble.
\item
  At \(c_n=1\), all interval hypotheses are identical and the exact-label
  baseline is \(1/M_n\). Near this endpoint, write \(M_n=n(n+1)/2\) and
  \(\tau_n=n(1-c_n)(\log n)^2\). Uniformly on compact
  \(\tau_n\)-windows,
  \(M_nP_X=[1+2\sqrt{\tau_n}/\pi+\sqrt2\,\tau_n/\pi^2]^2
  +O_T(\log\log n/\log n)\) for
  \(X\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{SRM},\mathrm{opt}\}\).
  On this scale \(n p_1(c_n)\) remains bounded. In the complementary
  hull-dominant regime \(c_n\to1\), \(c_n<1\) eventually, and
  \(n p_1(c_n)\to\infty\), we prove without monotonicity that
  \(P_X(G_n(c_n))\sim p_1(c_n)^2\) for all three \(X\). This includes the
  inner bridge, the continuum interface uniformly on compact parameter sets
  \(c_n=1-\lambda/n\), and the moving-outer regime
  \(n(1-c_n)\to\infty\).
\item
  Assigning \(H_0\) a fixed prior \(\pi_0\in(0,1)\), while retaining the
  corresponding uniform prior conditional on an anomaly, gives optimal joint
  limits \(\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)P_\infty(i,c^2)\) for fixed known \(i\),
  \(\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)p_1(c^2)\) for a growing known length, and
  \(\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)p_1(c)^2\) for unknown length. For every fixed
  \(0\leq c<1\), the prior-weighted SRM attains the latter two joint limits.
  At \(c=1\), its limiting success is \(\pi_0^2\), whereas a classical
  maximum-a-posteriori postprocessing restores the optimal limit \(\pi_0\).
\end{enumerate}

A singular-safe dual certificate and an exact weighted-hull decomposition are
the main technical tools. The certificate treats singular Gram ensembles
directly and removes the need for a uniform spectral bound in the
exceptional-sector transfer theorem. For moving overlaps, block and tail
estimates and adaptive endpoint macroblocks control the non-Toeplitz physical
weights.

The appearance of \(c^2\) in the growing known-length law and \(c\) in the
unknown-length comparison kernel reflects their different hypothesis
geometries. A growing interval of known length has one translation coordinate,
and a unit shift changes two local factors. With unknown length, the two
endpoints form separate coordinates, each contributing a kernel with parameter
\(c\). Finite-size semidefinite programs and direct SRM computations illustrate
the approach to these limits.

\paragraph{Assumptions and scope.}
We assume independent outputs conditional on the interval label, abrupt
switching between calibrated pure states, a constant anomalous state within
the interval, and exact return to \(\lvert0\rangle\). Except in
Section~\ref{a-nonuniform-boundary-at-unit-overlap}, the overlap \(c\) remains
fixed as \(n\to\infty\); \(\pi_0\) is fixed whenever \(H_0\) is included.
The stated uniform priors and unrestricted collective POVMs define the
decision problems. The fixed-overlap growing- and unknown-length limits are
pointwise in \(c\). Section~\ref{a-nonuniform-boundary-at-unit-overlap} treats
the ultracritical window and moving-overlap sequences under their stated
sufficient conditions. These results benchmark a receiver
capable of storing the full batch coherently and applying a collective
measurement. Extensions to nonideal outputs, including correlations, mixing,
gradual switching, or imperfect return, as well as unknown anomaly states,
restricted measurements, and multiple disjoint intervals, require separate
analysis and are discussed in Section~\ref{conclusion-and-discussion}.

The model is related to earlier quantum state discrimination problems in
precise ways. Its boundary slice \(b=n\) is the permanent change point family
\cite{sentis2016,sentis2017,sentis2018}. It is also a specialization of the
two-change framework of Mohan--Sikora--Upadhyay \cite{mohan2023};
Table~\ref{tab:literature} gives the exact parameter map and the common-factor
reduction that preserves the Gram matrix.  After fixing the phase of
\(\lvert\psi\rangle\), the three pairwise overlaps
are \((c,c,1)\), while \(c_1<c_2\) enforces a nonempty interval. For fixed
\(i\), the candidates form the consecutive \(k=i\) subensemble of quantum
multi-anomaly detection \cite{llorens2024}; the inclusion is strict for
\(1<i<n\), while the hypothesis sets coincide at \(i=1\) and \(i=n\). Quantum
edge detection instead has one boundary and unknown pure states in both
domains \cite{llorens2025}. Classical epidemic, changed-interval, and spatial
scan models are geometric analogues \cite{levin1985,yao1993,chen2015,walther2010},
but their criteria concern classical significance or power rather than exact
Bayes localization of generally noncommuting states by collective POVMs.
Table~\ref{tab:literature} summarizes the main structural distinctions.

\section{Model and preliminaries}\label{model-and-discrimination-preliminaries}

\subsection{Phase convention and Gram matrices}\label{phase-convention-and-gram-matrices}

Write

\[
\langle0|\psi\rangle=ce^{i\varphi},\qquad 0\le c\le1,
\qquad r=c^2.
\]

Replacing \(|\psi\rangle\) by \(e^{-i\varphi}|\psi\rangle\) changes each
candidate vector only by a global phase that depends on its label. The original
and phase-fixed Gram matrices are therefore related by diagonal unitary
conjugation, which leaves the minimum-error and SRM success probabilities
unchanged. We use

\[
\langle0|\psi\rangle=c\ge0
\]

without loss of generality.

Unless a theorem explicitly introduces a sequence \(i=i_n\), every
asymptotic statement is taken as \(n\to\infty\) with \(c\) (and hence
\(r=c^2\)) fixed. In Section~\ref{joint-detection-and-exact-localization},
the no-change prior \(\pi_0\in(0,1)\) is also fixed rather than scaled with
\(n\).

For an ensemble of \(M\) normalized pure states \(\{|\phi_j\rangle\}_{j=1}^M\) with equal priors, let

\[
G_{jk}=\langle\phi_j|\phi_k\rangle.
\]

We write \(P_{\rm opt}(G)\) for the optimal minimum-error success probability
over all POVMs
\cite{helstrom1976,holevo1973,yuen1975,barnett2009} and

\[
P_{\rm SRM}(G)=\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^M(\sqrt G)_{jj}^2
\]

for the square-root measurement success probability
\cite{hausladen1994,eldar2001,dallapozza2015}. If \(G\) is singular,
\(G^{-1/2}\) is understood on the support of \(G\), and the POVM is completed
arbitrarily on its orthogonal complement; this does not change any ensemble
success probability. The elementary trace bound

\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-srm-bound}
\left(\frac{\operatorname{tr}\sqrt G}{M}\right)^2
\le P_{\rm SRM}(G)\le P_{\rm opt}(G)
\end{equation}

will be used repeatedly.

\subsection{Stability under Gram perturbations}\label{stability-under-gram-perturbations}

\begin{lemma}[Operator-norm Gram stability]\label{lem:gram-stability}
Let \(G,H\in\mathbb C^{M\times M}\) be positive semidefinite with unit diagonal. Then

\begin{equation}\label{eq:opt-gram-stability}
|P_{\rm opt}(G)-P_{\rm opt}(H)|
\le \|\sqrt G-\sqrt H\|_{\rm op}
\le \|G-H\|_{\rm op}^{1/2}
\end{equation}

and

\begin{align}\label{eq:srm-gram-stability}
&|P_{\rm SRM}(G)-P_{\rm SRM}(H)| \notag\\
&\qquad\le2\|\sqrt G-\sqrt H\|_{\rm op}
\le2\|G-H\|_{\rm op}^{1/2}.
\end{align}

If \(G,H\ge \mu I\) for some \(\mu>0\), then

\begin{equation}\label{eq:sqrt-lipschitz}
\|\sqrt G-\sqrt H\|_{\rm op}
\le \frac{\|G-H\|_{\rm op}}{2\sqrt\mu}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the canonical Gram realizations \(|x_j\rangle=\sqrt G e_j\) and
\(|y_j\rangle=\sqrt H e_j\). Both vectors have unit norm because the Gram
matrices have unit diagonal, and the trace distance between the corresponding
pure states is at most \(\|x_j-y_j\|\). Evaluate an optimal POVM for either
ensemble on the other and average over \(j\) to obtain the first inequality in
\eqref{eq:opt-gram-stability}. The second is the operator Hölder inequality
for positive square roots. Since
\(0\le(\sqrt G)_{jj},(\sqrt H)_{jj}\le1\), the scalar bound
\(|a^2-b^2|\le2|a-b|\) gives \eqref{eq:srm-gram-stability}. To prove
\eqref{eq:sqrt-lipschitz}, \(X=\sqrt G-\sqrt H\) solves

\[
\sqrt G X+X\sqrt H=G-H.
\]

The inverse Sylvester operator has norm at most \((2\sqrt\mu)^{-1}\), which proves \eqref{eq:sqrt-lipschitz}.
\end{proof}

\section{A known interval length}\label{a-known-interval-length}

Let the anomalous interval have known length \(i\ge1\). There are

\[
N=n-i+1
\]

admissible starting points, and the equal-prior candidates are

\begin{align*}
|\Phi_a^{(i)}\rangle
={}&|0\rangle^{\otimes(a-1)}|\psi\rangle^{\otimes i}
|0\rangle^{\otimes(n-a-i+1)}, \\
&a=1,\ldots,N.
\end{align*}

Their exact Gram matrix is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-gram}
(G_{N,i})_{ab}=r^{\min(|a-b|,i)}.
\end{equation}

Define

\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-symbol}
f_{i,r}(\theta)
=1-r^i+2\sum_{d=1}^{i-1}(r^d-r^i)\cos(d\theta).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Fixed-length limit}\label{fixed-length-limit}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-length localization]\label{thm:fixed-length}
For every fixed integer \(i\ge1\) and \(0\le r\le1\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-limit}
\lim_{N\to\infty}P_{\rm opt}(G_{N,i})
=\lim_{N\to\infty}P_{\rm SRM}(G_{N,i})
=P_\infty(i,r),
\end{equation}

where

\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-integral}
P_\infty(i,r)
=\left[
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\sqrt{f_{i,r}(\theta)}\,d\theta
\right]^2.
\end{equation}

For fixed \(i\) and \(0<r<1\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-gap-rate}
0\le P_{\rm opt}(G_{N,i})-P_{\rm SRM}(G_{N,i})
=O_{i,r}(N^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Residual gap.}
Put \(q=r^i\) and decompose

\[
G_{N,i}=qJ_N+B_{N,i},
\]

where \(B_{N,i}=T_N(f_{i,r})\) has entries

\[
(B_{N,i})_{ab}=
\begin{cases}
r^{|a-b|}-r^i,&|a-b|<i,\\
0,&|a-b|\ge i.
\end{cases}
\]

Choose a local basis with
\(|\psi\rangle=\sqrt r\,|0\rangle+\sqrt{1-r}\,|1\rangle\). After removing the
common all-zero component, each candidate has a unique basis component with
squared amplitude \((1-r)^i\). Hence

\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-spectral-gap}
B_{N,i}\ge(1-r)^iI_N.
\end{equation}

The corresponding infinite residual convolution operator has symbol
\(f_{i,r}\). Applying \eqref{eq:residual-spectral-gap} to arbitrarily long
finitely supported vectors gives

\begin{equation}\label{eq:symbol-spectral-gap}
f_{i,r}(\theta)\ge(1-r)^i.
\end{equation}

\emph{Circulant comparison.}
For \(N\ge2i\), let \(\widetilde B_{N,i}\) be the circulant matrix with the same local coefficients and set

\[
C_{N,i}=r^iJ_N+\widetilde B_{N,i}.
\]

Away from the uniform Fourier mode, its eigenvalues are the sampled values
\(f_{i,r}(2\pi k/N)\). The uniform mode receives an additional positive
rank-one contribution. Thus \(C_{N,i}\) is a positive definite, unit-diagonal
circulant Gram matrix. The matrices \(B_{N,i}\) and \(\widetilde B_{N,i}\)
differ only in the upper-right and lower-left wraparound corner blocks.

To make the boundary estimate explicit, set

\begin{align*}
b_d&=r^d-r^i,\\
E_{N,d}&=\sum_{a=1}^{d}e_a e_{N-d+a}^*,
\qquad 1\le d<i.
\end{align*}

Here \(E_{N,d}\) is a rank-\(d\) partial permutation from the last \(d\)
coordinates to the first \(d\) coordinates, and

\begin{equation}\label{eq:corner-partial-permutation}
\widetilde B_{N,i}-B_{N,i}
=\sum_{d=1}^{i-1}b_d(E_{N,d}+E_{N,d}^*).
\end{equation}

For \(N\ge2i\), the initial and terminal coordinate subspaces in
\eqref{eq:corner-partial-permutation} are disjoint. Consequently
\(\|E_{N,d}+E_{N,d}^*\|_1=2d\). Moreover, the whole difference maps between
the span of the first \(i-1\) coordinates and the span of the last \(i-1\)
coordinates. The triangle inequality and this two-corner support therefore give

\begin{equation}\label{eq:circulant-trace-perturbation}
\|G_{N,i}-C_{N,i}\|_1
\le K_{i,r}:=2\sum_{d=1}^{i-1}d(r^d-r^i),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:circulant-rank-perturbation}
\operatorname{rank}(G_{N,i}-C_{N,i})\le2(i-1).
\end{equation}

The Sylvester integral gives

\begin{equation}\label{eq:sqrt-trace-perturbation}
\|\sqrt{G_{N,i}}-\sqrt{C_{N,i}}\|_1
\le\frac{K_{i,r}}{2(1-r)^{i/2}}=O_{i,r}(1).
\end{equation}

\emph{Exact circulant optimum.}
Because \(C_{N,i}\) and \(\sqrt{C_{N,i}}\) are circulant, the diagonal of \(\sqrt{C_{N,i}}\) is constant. If

\[
\beta_N=\frac1N\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{C_{N,i}},
\]

then the SRM value is \(\beta_N^2\). The Yuen--Kennedy--Lax dual operator

\[
Y=\frac{\beta_N}{N}\sqrt{C_{N,i}}
\]

is feasible because, with \(S=\sqrt{C_{N,i}}\),

\[
\beta_NS-Se_je_j^*S\ge0.
\]

Indeed, since \(S\succ0\), congruence by \(S^{-1/2}\) reduces this to

\[
\beta_N I-S^{1/2}e_je_j^*S^{1/2}\ge0.
\]

The rank-one term has the unique nonzero eigenvalue
\(e_j^*Se_j=\beta_N\), because the diagonal of \(S\) is constant. Since
\(\operatorname{tr}Y=\beta_N^2\), the SRM is exactly optimal for the
circulant model.

\emph{Transfer and spectral limit.}

Let

\[
\tau_N=\|\sqrt{G_{N,i}}-\sqrt{C_{N,i}}\|_1.
\]

Put \(\Delta_N=\sqrt{G_{N,i}}-\sqrt{C_{N,i}}\). The canonical Gram realizations, followed by Cauchy--Schwarz and the monotonicity of Schatten norms, give the explicit chain

\begin{align}\label{eq:opt-circulant-transfer}
|P_{\rm opt}(G_{N,i})-P_{\rm opt}(C_{N,i})|
&\le\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\|\Delta_Ne_j\|_2 \notag\\
&\le\frac1{\sqrt N}
\left(\sum_{j=1}^N\|\Delta_Ne_j\|_2^2\right)^{1/2} \notag\\
&=\frac{\|\Delta_N\|_{\rm HS}}{\sqrt N}
\notag\\
&\le\frac{\|\Delta_N\|_1}{\sqrt N}
=\frac{\tau_N}{\sqrt N}.
\end{align}

while the SRM diagonal formula gives

\[
|P_{\rm SRM}(G_{N,i})-P_{\rm SRM}(C_{N,i})|
\le\frac{2\tau_N}{N}.
\]

Together with \eqref{eq:sqrt-trace-perturbation}, this proves
\eqref{eq:fixed-gap-rate}. Finally, the sampled eigenvalues form a Riemann
sum. The rank-one correction to the uniform mode changes
\(N^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\sqrt C\) by at most \(O(N^{-1/2})\). Therefore

\[
\frac1N\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{C_{N,i}}
\longrightarrow
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\sqrt{f_{i,r}(\theta)}\,d\theta.
\]

This proves \eqref{eq:fixed-limit}--\eqref{eq:fixed-integral} for \(0<r<1\). The cases \(r=0,1\) are handled below.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Residual Toeplitz family]\label{cor:residual-toeplitz}
Fix \(i\ge1\) and \(0\le r<1\), put \(q=r^i\), and let

\[
R_{N,i}:=\frac{G_{N,i}-qJ_N}{1-q}
=T_N\!\left(\frac{f_{i,r}}{1-r^i}\right).
\]

Then \(R_{N,i}\) is the Gram matrix of normalized residual states and

\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-toeplitz-limit}
\lim_{N\to\infty}P_{\rm opt}(R_{N,i})
=\lim_{N\to\infty}P_{\rm SRM}(R_{N,i})
=\frac{P_\infty(i,r)}{1-r^i}.
\end{equation}

Equivalently,
\((1-r^i)P_{\rm opt}(R_{N,i})\to P_\infty(i,r)\), and the same statement
holds with \(P_{\rm SRM}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The diagonal of \(G_{N,i}-qJ_N\) is \(1-q\), and
\eqref{eq:residual-spectral-gap} shows that it is positive definite. Thus
\(R_{N,i}\) is a unit-diagonal Gram matrix. Its Toeplitz symbol is
\(h_{i,r}=f_{i,r}/(1-r^i)\), which is bounded above and below away from zero.
The one-dimensional instance of Lemma~\ref{lem:folner-diagonal}, followed by
Lemma~\ref{lem:gram-upper-bound} and \eqref{eq:trace-srm-bound}, gives the common
limit

\[
\left[\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\sqrt{h_{i,r}(\theta)}\,d\theta\right]^2
=\frac{P_\infty(i,r)}{1-r^i}.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact and special-function cases}\label{exact-and-special-function-cases}

\begin{corollary}[One-site interval, finite size]\label{cor:one-site}
For \(i=1\),

\[
G_{N,1}=(1-r)I_N+rJ_N
\]

is permutation invariant. Its positive square root has constant diagonal, so
the preceding dual certificate proves finite-size SRM optimality. Its
eigenvalues are \(1+(N-1)r\) once and \(1-r\) with multiplicity \(N-1\), so

\begin{align}\label{eq:one-site-finite}
P_{\rm opt}(G_{N,1})
&=P_{\rm SRM}(G_{N,1}) \notag\\
&=\frac{\bigl[\sqrt{1+(N-1)r}
+(N-1)\sqrt{1-r}\bigr]^2}{N^2}.
\end{align}

In particular, \(P_\infty(1,r)=1-r\).

This is the \(k=1\) specialization of the known single-anomaly result in
Refs.~\cite{skotiniotis2024,llorens2024}, after identifying their common
off-diagonal Gram entry with \(r=c^2\). Thus the interval model reproduces the
established finite-size formula at \(i=1\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Two-site interval]\label{cor:two-site}
With

\[
E(m)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\sqrt{1-m\sin^2u}\,du,
\]

one has

\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-site-elliptic}
P_\infty(2,r)
=\frac4{\pi^2}(1-r)(1+3r)
E^2\!\left(\frac{4r}{1+3r}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Derivation]
Equation~\eqref{eq:fixed-symbol} becomes

\begin{align*}
f_{2,r}(\theta)
&=(1-r)(1+r+2r\cos\theta) \\
&=(1-r)(1+3r-4r\sin^2(\theta/2)).
\end{align*}

With \(u=\theta/2\) and symmetry about \(\pi/2\),

\begin{align*}
&\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\sqrt{f_{2,r}(\theta)}\,d\theta \\
&\qquad=\frac2\pi\sqrt{(1-r)(1+3r)}
E\!\left(\frac{4r}{1+3r}\right).
\end{align*}

Squaring proves \eqref{eq:two-site-elliptic}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Endpoint parameters}\label{endpoint-parameters}

If \(r=0\), then \(G_{N,i}=I_N\), and both success probabilities equal one.
If \(r=1\), then \(G_{N,i}=J_N\), all hypotheses represent the same ray, and
both probabilities equal \(1/N\). These values agree with the continuous
limiting formulas. The spectral-gap arguments above apply only for
\(0<r<1\).

\section{A growing known length}\label{a-growing-known-length}

Define the Poisson kernel

\[
g_r(\theta)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z}r^{|d|}e^{id\theta}
=\frac{1-r^2}{1-2r\cos\theta+r^2}
\]

and

\begin{equation}\label{eq:p1-definition}
p_1(r)=\left[\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\sqrt{g_r(\theta)}\,d\theta\right]^2
=\frac{4(1-r^2)}{\pi^2}K^2(r^2),
\end{equation}

where \(K\) is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind in the
parameter convention, and \(p_1(1)=0\) by continuity. The last equality follows
from the standard Landen transformation. All proofs use the integral
representation as the definition.

\begin{proposition}[One change Toeplitz benchmark]\label{prop:one-change-benchmark}
For

\[
Q_N=(r^{|a-b|})_{a,b=1}^N,
\qquad 0\le r<1,
\]

one has

\begin{align}\label{eq:one-change-limit}
P_{\rm opt}(Q_N),P_{\rm SRM}(Q_N)
&\longrightarrow p_1(r), \notag\\
\frac1N\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{Q_N}
&\longrightarrow\sqrt{p_1(r)}.
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The matrix \(Q_N\) is the compression of the one-dimensional convolution
operator with kernel \(r^{|d|}\), whose symbol is \(g_r\) and is bounded above
and below away from zero. Lemma~\ref{lem:folner-diagonal}, with \(d=1\) and
\(\phi(t)=\sqrt t\), gives convergence in mean of the square-root diagonal to
\((2\pi)^{-1}\int\sqrt{g_r}=\sqrt{p_1(r)}\). Lemma~\ref{lem:gram-upper-bound}
supplies the matching minimum-error upper bound, while
\eqref{eq:trace-srm-bound} supplies the lower bound and the SRM squeeze. This
recovers the one-dimensional change-point Toeplitz functional of
Ref.~\cite{sentis2016}, evaluated here at the effective compound overlap
\(r=c^2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Growing known interval length]\label{thm:long-known}
Let \(i=i_n\) and \(N_n=n-i_n+1\). If

\begin{equation}\label{eq:long-known-conditions}
i_n\to\infty,\qquad N_n\to\infty,
\end{equation}

then, for every fixed \(0\le r\le1\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:long-known-limit}
P_{\rm opt}(G_{N_n,i_n}),
P_{\rm SRM}(G_{N_n,i_n})
\longrightarrow p_1(r)=p_1(c^2).
\end{equation}

No condition on \(i_n/N_n\) is required.
The convergence is pointwise in fixed \(r\). No finite-size rate or
uniformity as \(r\uparrow1\) is claimed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The endpoint values \(r=0,1\) follow from the matrices described in
Section~\ref{endpoint-parameters}. We therefore assume \(0<r<1\) and put
\(q_i=r^i\). Separating the common all zero component gives

\[
|\Phi_a^{(i)}\rangle
=\sqrt{q_i}|\Omega\rangle
+\sqrt{1-q_i}|\xi_a^{(i)}\rangle,
\qquad \langle\Omega|\xi_a^{(i)}\rangle=0.
\]

The normalized residual Gram matrix is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-gram}
(R_{N,i})_{ab}=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{r^{|a-b|}-r^i}{1-r^i},&|a-b|<i,\\
0,&|a-b|\ge i.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

Its symbol

\[
h_{i,r}(\theta)
=1+2\sum_{d=1}^{i-1}\frac{r^d-r^i}{1-r^i}\cos(d\theta)
\]

satisfies

\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-symbol-convergence}
\|h_{i,r}-g_r\|_\infty
\le\frac{2(i-1)r^i}{1-r^i}+\frac{2r^i}{1-r}
=:\varepsilon_i(r)\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}

Let \(Q_N=(r^{|a-b|})_{a,b=1}^N\). Toeplitz compression gives

\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-operator-convergence}
\|R_{N,i}-Q_N\|_{\rm op}\le\varepsilon_i(r).
\end{equation}

Since \(g_r\ge(1-r)/(1+r)>0\), both matrices have a common positive
spectral gap for large \(i\). Lemma~\ref{lem:gram-stability} therefore
transfers the optimal minimum-error success probability uniformly in \(N\).
The trace distance
between each original state and its residual state is also
\(\sqrt{q_i}=r^{i/2}\), so

\[
|P_{\rm opt}(G_{N,i})-P_{\rm opt}(R_{N,i})|
\le r^{i/2}.
\]

The single-change-point Toeplitz limit for \(Q_N\) therefore proves

\[
P_{\rm opt}(G_{N,i})\to p_1(r).
\]

For the SRM, the rank one common component requires a separate trace estimate.
Since

\[
G_{N,i}=q_iJ_N+(1-q_i)R_{N,i},
\]

operator monotonicity and the Rotfel'd trace inequality give the exact sandwich

\begin{align}\label{eq:rank-one-sandwich}
\sqrt{1-q_i}\,\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{R_{N,i}}
&\le\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{G_{N,i}} \notag\\
&\le\sqrt{1-q_i}\,\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{R_{N,i}}
+\sqrt{q_iN}.
\end{align}

Equation~\eqref{eq:residual-operator-convergence}, the common spectral gap, and the Toeplitz limit imply

\[
\frac1N\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{R_{N,i}}
\longrightarrow\sqrt{p_1(r)}.
\]

After dividing \eqref{eq:rank-one-sandwich} by \(N\), the remaining rank-one term is \(\sqrt{q_i/N}\to0\). Thus

\[
\frac1N\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{G_{N,i}}
\longrightarrow\sqrt{p_1(r)}.
\]

The sandwich \eqref{eq:trace-srm-bound} completes the SRM proof.
\end{proof}

fixed-length limits $P_\infty(i,c^2)$; as $i$ grows these limits
approach the long-interval law $p_1(c^2)$ derived in
Theorem~\ref{thm:long-known}.

If \(i\ge N-1\), then \(\min(|a-b|,i)=|a-b|\) for every matrix entry, and hence

\[
G_{N,i}=Q_N
\]

exactly. The approximation in Theorem~\ref{thm:long-known} is needed only when \(i<N-1\).
\end{remark}

\section{Unknown interval length}\label{unknown-interval-length}

Let

\begin{align*}
\mathcal I_n&=\{[a,b]:1\le a\le b\le n\}, \\
M_n&=|\mathcal I_n|=\frac{n(n+1)}2,
\end{align*}

and equip \(\mathcal I_n\) with the uniform prior \(1/M_n\). This is uniform
over intervals, not over lengths. If \(L=|I|\), the induced marginal is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:induced-length-prior}
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\{L=\ell\}
&=\frac{n-\ell+1}{M_n}\\
&=\frac{2(n-\ell+1)}{n(n+1)},
\qquad 1\leq\ell\leq n.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Conditionally on \(L=\ell\), the starting point is uniform over its
\(n-\ell+1\) admissible values. Consequently
\(\mathbb E L=(n+2)/3\), and \(L/n\) converges to the triangular density
\(2(1-x)\) on \(0<x<1\). Thus the prior favors shorter lengths, but its
typical interval still has order-\(n\) length. The candidate indexed by
\(I=[a,b]\) has \(|\psi\rangle\) on \(I\) and \(|0\rangle\) elsewhere. Its
exact Gram kernel is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:interval-gram}
G^{(n)}_{I,J}=c^{|I\triangle J|}.
\end{equation}

Theorem~\ref{thm:unknown-length} is therefore a Bayes law under the uniform
interval prior in \eqref{eq:induced-length-prior}. Other priors may retain
nonvanishing mass on bounded or logarithmically short intervals and can lead
to different limiting reductions.

For intersecting intervals,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:intersecting-distance}
|I\triangle J|=|a-a'|+|b-b'|.
\end{equation}

For disjoint intervals, assume without loss of generality that \(b<a'\) and define the separating gap by

\[
g(I,J):=a'-b-1\ge0.
\]

Then the exact identity is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:disjoint-distance}
|a-a'|+|b-b'|=|I\triangle J|+2g(I,J).
\end{equation}

Thus \eqref{eq:intersecting-distance} still holds for adjacent intervals,
where \(g(I,J)=0\), but differs by twice the separating gap when
\(g(I,J)>0\). The exact physical kernel is therefore not globally a
two-dimensional Toeplitz kernel.

Classical multilevel Szeg{\H o} theorems describe spectral averages for genuine
multilevel Toeplitz compressions \cite{tilli1998,tyrtyshnikov1998}. Here the
triangular endpoint sets and, more importantly, the non-Toeplitz physical
kernel require a local diagonal Følner statement and a separate perturbative
transfer back to \(G^{(n)}\).

\subsection{A Følner functional calculus lemma}\label{a-folner-functional-calculus-lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Local diagonal Følner limit]\label{lem:folner-diagonal}
Let \(k\in\ell^1(\mathbb Z^d)\) satisfy \(k(-u)=\overline{k(u)}\), let \(A\) be convolution by \(k\) on \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z^d)\), and suppose its Fourier symbol satisfies

\[
0<\mu\le F(\theta)\le L<\infty
\]

almost everywhere. If \(\Lambda_n\subset\mathbb Z^d\) is any finite Følner sequence and \(A_n=P_nAP_n|_{\ell^2(\Lambda_n)}\), then for every continuous \(\phi:[\mu,L]\to\mathbb C\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:folner-diagonal}
\frac1{|\Lambda_n|}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_n}
\left|
\langle\delta_x,\phi(A_n)\delta_x\rangle-a_\phi
\right|\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}

where

\[
a_\phi=\frac1{(2\pi)^d}\int_{[0,2\pi]^d}\phi(F(\theta))\,d\theta.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(A\) has spectrum in \([\mu,L]\), the same is true of every compression
\(A_n\). Fix \(\varepsilon>0\) and put \(\eta=\varepsilon/8\). By the
Weierstrass theorem, applied to the real and imaginary parts if necessary,
choose a polynomial

\[
p(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{s}a_mt^m
\quad\text{such that}\quad
\sup_{t\in[\mu,L]}|\phi(t)-p(t)|<\eta.
\]

Let \(k_R=k\,\mathbf 1_{\{\|u\|_\infty\le R\}}\), let \(A_R\) be convolution
by \(k_R\), and set \(A_{R,n}=P_nA_RP_n|_{\ell^2(\Lambda_n)}\). Young's
inequality gives

\begin{equation}\label{eq:folner-young}
\|A-A_R\|_{\rm op},\ \|A_n-A_{R,n}\|_{\rm op}
\le\|k-k_R\|_{\ell^1}.
\end{equation}

Choose \(R\) so large that the right hand side of
\eqref{eq:folner-young} is at most \(1\) and

\[
C_p\|k-k_R\|_{\ell^1}<\eta,\qquad
C_p:=\sum_{m=1}^{s}m|a_m|(L+1)^{m-1},
\]

where the second condition is void if \(C_p=0\). The telescoping identity for
\(X^m-Y^m\), together with
\(\|A\|,\|A_n\|\le L\) and
\(\|A_R\|,\|A_{R,n}\|\le L+1\), then yields

\begin{equation}\label{eq:folner-polynomial-truncation}
\|p(A)-p(A_R)\|_{\rm op},
\ \|p(A_n)-p(A_{R,n})\|_{\rm op}<\eta.
\end{equation}

Define

\[
Q_t=\{h\in\mathbb Z^d:\|h\|_\infty\le t\}
\]

and the \(sR\)-interior and boundary by

\begin{align*}
\Lambda_n^{\circ,sR}
&=\{x\in\Lambda_n:x+Q_{sR}\subseteq\Lambda_n\},\\
\partial_{sR}\Lambda_n
&=\Lambda_n\setminus\Lambda_n^{\circ,sR}.
\end{align*}

Because a degree \(s\) polynomial in a range \(R\) operator has propagation
radius \(sR\), every length \(m\le s\) path generated by the kernel and
starting at \(x\in\Lambda_n^{\circ,sR}\) stays in \(\Lambda_n\). Therefore

\begin{equation}\label{eq:folner-interior-equality}
\langle\delta_x,p(A_{R,n})\delta_x\rangle
=\langle\delta_0,p(A_R)\delta_0\rangle,
\qquad x\in\Lambda_n^{\circ,sR}.
\end{equation}

Moreover,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:folner-boundary-inclusion}
\partial_{sR}\Lambda_n
\subseteq
\bigcup_{\|h\|_\infty\le sR}
\bigl(\Lambda_n\setminus(\Lambda_n-h)\bigr).
\end{equation}

The union is finite and each summand has \(o(|\Lambda_n|)\) elements by the
Følner property. Hence
\(|\partial_{sR}\Lambda_n|/|\Lambda_n|\to0\).
With
\(K_p=\sup_{|t|\le L+1}|p(t)|\), define

\begin{align*}
d_{n,R,p}(x)
&:=
\left|
\langle\delta_x,p(A_{R,n})\delta_x\rangle
-\langle\delta_0,p(A_R)\delta_0\rangle
\right|,\\
D_{n,R,p}
&:=\frac1{|\Lambda_n|}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_n}d_{n,R,p}(x).
\end{align*}

Equations
\eqref{eq:folner-interior-equality}--\eqref{eq:folner-boundary-inclusion} give

\begin{equation}\label{eq:folner-boundary-average}
D_{n,R,p}
\le2K_p\frac{|\partial_{sR}\Lambda_n|}{|\Lambda_n|}
\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}

Finally, the spectral theorem and Fourier transformation identify

\[
\langle\delta_0,\phi(A)\delta_0\rangle
=\frac1{(2\pi)^d}\int_{[0,2\pi]^d}\phi(F(\theta))\,d\theta
=a_\phi.
\]

Put

\[
D_n^\phi:=\frac1{|\Lambda_n|}\sum_{x\in\Lambda_n}
\left|\langle\delta_x,\phi(A_n)\delta_x\rangle-a_\phi\right|.
\]

For all sufficiently large \(n\), functional calculus,
\eqref{eq:folner-polynomial-truncation}, and
\eqref{eq:folner-boundary-average} yield

\begin{align*}
D_n^\phi
&\le
\|\phi(A_n)-p(A_n)\|_{\rm op}\\
&\quad+\|p(A_n)-p(A_{R,n})\|_{\rm op}\\
&\quad+D_{n,R,p}\\
&\quad+\|p(A_R)-p(A)\|_{\rm op}\\
&\quad+\|p(A)-\phi(A)\|_{\rm op}\\
&<4\eta+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon.
\end{align*}

This proves \eqref{eq:folner-diagonal}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Singular-safe Gram dual bound]\label{lem:gram-upper-bound}
Let \(G\) be an \(M\times M\) positive semidefinite Gram matrix with unit
diagonal. Set

\[
\begin{aligned}
X&=\sqrt G, & d_j&=X_{jj},\\
\bar d&=\frac{\operatorname{tr}X}{M},&
|x_j\rangle&=X|e_j\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]

Then \(\bar d>0\), and the operator

\begin{equation}\label{eq:singular-safe-dual-certificate}
Y_G=\frac1M\left[
\bar d X+
\sum_{j:d_j>\bar d}
\left(1-\frac{\bar d}{d_j}\right)
|x_j\rangle\langle x_j|
\right]
\end{equation}

is feasible for the minimum-error dual problem. Consequently,

\begin{align}\label{eq:gram-upper-bound}
\bar d^2
&\le P_{\rm SRM}(G)
\le P_{\rm opt}(G) \notag\\
&\le
\operatorname{tr}Y_G \notag\\
&=\bar d^2+
\frac1M\sum_{j:d_j>\bar d}
\left(1-\frac{\bar d}{d_j}\right) \notag\\
&\le \bar d^2+
\frac1{2M\bar d}\sum_{j=1}^M|d_j-\bar d|.
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The lower bound is \eqref{eq:trace-srm-bound}. Since
\(\operatorname{tr}G=M>0\), also \(\bar d>0\). Put
\(\rho_j=|x_j\rangle\langle x_j|\). For every vector \(|v\rangle\),
Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\begin{align*}
\langle v|\rho_j|v\rangle
&=|\langle e_j|Xv\rangle|^2\\
&=|\langle G^{1/4}e_j|G^{1/4}v\rangle|^2\\
&\le d_j\langle v|X|v\rangle.
\end{align*}

Thus \(\rho_j\le d_jX\), without requiring \(G\) to be invertible. If
\(d_j\le\bar d\), the first term in \(MY_G\) already dominates \(\rho_j\).
If \(d_j>\bar d\), then

\[
\bar d X\ge\frac{\bar d}{d_j}\rho_j,
\]

and the \(j\)th correction in \eqref{eq:singular-safe-dual-certificate}
supplies the remaining multiple of \(\rho_j\). All other corrections are
positive semidefinite. Hence \(Y_G\ge\rho_j/M\) for every \(j\), so \(Y_G\)
is dual feasible. Finally, \(\operatorname{tr}\rho_j=G_{jj}=1\), and for
\(d_j>\bar d\),

\[
1-\frac{\bar d}{d_j}
=\frac{d_j-\bar d}{d_j}
\le\frac{|d_j-\bar d|}{\bar d}.
\]

The canonical equal-prior dual constraints are \(Y\ge\rho_j/M\); weak
duality therefore gives \(P_{\rm opt}(G)\le\operatorname{tr}Y_G\). Finally,
\(\sum_j(d_j-\bar d)=0\), so the sum of the positive deviations is one half
of \(\sum_j|d_j-\bar d|\). Taking the trace of
\eqref{eq:singular-safe-dual-certificate} proves the remaining bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exceptional-sector Gram transfer]
\label{thm:exceptional-sector-transfer}
For each \(n\), let \(G_n\) be an \(M_n\times M_n\) positive semidefinite
Gram matrix with unit diagonal. Let \(\mathcal B_n\) be a set of \(m_n\)
labels, let \(G_{n,\mathcal B}\) be the associated principal submatrix, and
let \(H_n\) be an \(m_n\times m_n\) positive semidefinite matrix with unit
diagonal. Suppose

\begin{align}
\frac{m_n}{M_n}&\longrightarrow1,
\label{eq:transfer-mass}\\
\|G_{n,\mathcal B}-H_n\|_{\rm op}&\longrightarrow0,
\label{eq:transfer-gram}
\end{align}

and, for some \(\alpha>0\),

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:transfer-local-diagonal}
\frac1{m_n}\sum_{j=1}^{m_n}
\left|(\sqrt{H_n})_{jj}-\alpha\right|\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}

Then the complete equiprobable ensemble satisfies

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:transfer-conclusion}
P_{\rm opt}(G_n),P_{\rm SRM}(G_n)\longrightarrow\alpha^2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(d_{n,j}=(\sqrt{H_n})_{jj}\) and
\(\bar d_n=m_n^{-1}\sum_jd_{n,j}\). Positivity and the unit diagonal give
\(0\le d_{n,j}\le1\), because

\[
d_{n,j}^2
\le\langle e_j,H_ne_j\rangle=1.
\]

Equation~\eqref{eq:transfer-local-diagonal} implies
\(\bar d_n\to\alpha\) and

\[
\frac1{m_n}\sum_jd_{n,j}^2\longrightarrow\alpha^2.
\]

The latter is \(P_{\rm SRM}(H_n)\). Put

\[
a_n=\frac1{m_n}\sum_j|d_{n,j}-\alpha|.
\]

Then \(|\bar d_n-\alpha|\le a_n\to0\), so \(\bar d_n\ge\alpha/2\) for all
sufficiently large \(n\), and

\[
\frac1{2m_n\bar d_n}\sum_j|d_{n,j}-\bar d_n|
\le\frac{a_n}{\bar d_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]

The singular-safe dual certificate in Lemma~\ref{lem:gram-upper-bound} now
gives

\[
P_{\rm opt}(H_n)\longrightarrow\alpha^2.
\]

By Lemma~\ref{lem:gram-stability} and
\eqref{eq:transfer-gram}, the retained physical family has the same limits:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:transfer-retained-limits}
P_{\rm opt}(G_{n,\mathcal B}),
P_{\rm SRM}(G_{n,\mathcal B})\longrightarrow\alpha^2.
\end{equation}

The same square root perturbation bound also yields

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:transfer-retained-trace}
\frac{\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{G_{n,\mathcal B}}}{m_n}
\longrightarrow\alpha.
\end{equation}

Write \(\beta_n=m_n/M_n\). Restricting a full ensemble POVM to the retained
labels and completing the resulting sub POVM gives the upper bound below;
extending an optimal retained family POVM gives the lower bound:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:transfer-optimum-sandwich}
\beta_nP_{\rm opt}(G_{n,\mathcal B})
\le P_{\rm opt}(G_n)
\le\beta_nP_{\rm opt}(G_{n,\mathcal B})+(1-\beta_n).
\end{equation}

Equations~\eqref{eq:transfer-mass},
\eqref{eq:transfer-retained-limits}, and
\eqref{eq:transfer-optimum-sandwich} prove
\(P_{\rm opt}(G_n)\to\alpha^2\).

It remains to transfer the SRM. Put

\[
t_n=\frac{\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{G_n}}{M_n}.
\]

Eigenvalue interlacing for the principal submatrix gives

\[
t_n\ge
\beta_n\frac{\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{G_{n,\mathcal B}}}{m_n}
\longrightarrow\alpha.
\]

On the other hand, \(t_n^2\le P_{\rm opt}(G_n)\to\alpha^2\), so
\(t_n\to\alpha\). The universal squeeze

\[
t_n^2\le P_{\rm SRM}(G_n)\le P_{\rm opt}(G_n)
\]

proves \eqref{eq:transfer-conclusion}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Følner comparison transfer]
\label{cor:folner-comparison-transfer}
Let \(k\in\ell^1(\mathbb Z^d)\) be Hermitian with \(k(0)=1\), and suppose
the symbol \(F\) of its convolution operator satisfies
\(0<\mu\le F\le L<\infty\). Let \(\Lambda_n\) be a finite Følner sequence
and \(H_n\) the compression of this convolution operator to
\(\ell^2(\Lambda_n)\). If \(G_n\) and retained label sets
\(\mathcal B_n\) satisfy

\[
\frac{|\mathcal B_n|}{M_n}\to1,
\qquad
\|G_n[\mathcal B_n]-H_n\|_{\rm op}\to0,
\]

define

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:folner-transfer-alpha}
\alpha_F=\frac1{(2\pi)^d}
\int_{[0,2\pi]^d}\sqrt{F(\theta)}\,d\theta.
\end{equation}

Then

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:folner-transfer-conclusion}
P_{\rm opt}(G_n),P_{\rm SRM}(G_n)
\longrightarrow\alpha_F^2.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The matrices \(H_n\) have unit diagonal. Lemma~\ref{lem:folner-diagonal}, applied to
\(\phi(t)=\sqrt t\), gives
\eqref{eq:transfer-local-diagonal} with

\[
\alpha=\alpha_F>0.
\]

Theorem~\ref{thm:exceptional-sector-transfer} applies.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The two-endpoint law}\label{the-two-endpoint-law}

\begin{theorem}[Unknown length interval]\label{thm:unknown-length}
Under the uniform prior on \(\mathcal I_n\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:unknown-length-limit}
P_{\rm opt}(G^{(n)}),P_{\rm SRM}(G^{(n)})
\longrightarrow p_1(c)^2
\end{equation}

for every fixed \(0\le c\le1\).
The convergence is pointwise in fixed \(c\). No finite-size rate or
uniformity as \(c\uparrow1\) follows from this theorem; the separate
moving-overlap laws are treated in
Section~\ref{a-nonuniform-boundary-at-unit-overlap}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Retained family.}
For \(c=0\), the hypotheses are orthogonal, whereas for \(c=1\) they are
identical; both cases agree with the stated limit. Assume henceforth that
\(0<c<1\), and choose

\[
L_n=\lceil\gamma\log n\rceil,
\qquad \gamma>|\log c|^{-1},
\]

and retain only

\[
\mathcal B_n=\{I\in\mathcal I_n:|I|\ge L_n\}.
\]

The deleted prior mass is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:short-interval-mass}
\varepsilon_n
=1-\frac{|\mathcal B_n|}{M_n}
=O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)\to0.
\end{equation}

On \(\mathcal B_n\), define the comparison kernel

\begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison-kernel}
H^{(n)}_{I,J}=c^{|a-a'|+|b-b'|}.
\end{equation}

It is the compression of the positive convolution kernel \(c^{|u|+|v|}\). The two kernels agree for intersecting intervals. For disjoint intervals,

\[
|G_{I,J}-H_{I,J}|
\le c^{|I|+|J|}\le c^{2L_n}.
\]

Consequently,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison-operator}
\|G_{\mathcal B_n}-H_{\mathcal B_n}\|_{\rm op}
\le|\mathcal B_n|c^{2L_n}=o(1).
\end{equation}

\emph{Følner geometry.}
The retained endpoint sets form a Følner sequence. Under the endpoint
identification \(I=[a,b]\leftrightarrow(a,b)\),

\[
\mathcal B_n
=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2:a\ge1,\ b\le n,\ b-a\ge L_n-1\},
\]

and

\begin{equation}\label{eq:long-domain-cardinality}
|\mathcal B_n|
=\frac{(n-L_n+1)(n-L_n+2)}2
\sim\frac{n^2}{2}.
\end{equation}

Fix \(h=(u,v)\in\mathbb Z^2\) and put \(H=\|h\|_\infty\). If the indicators
of \(\mathcal B_n\) at \(x=(a,b)\) and \(x+h\) differ, at least one of the
three affine inequalities defining \(\mathcal B_n\) changes truth value.
Such an \(x\) lies in a strip of respectively \(|u|\), \(|v|\), or
\(|v-u|\) lattice layers adjacent to
\(a=1\), \(b=n\), or \(b-a=L_n-1\). Because either \(x\) or \(x+h\) belongs
to \(\mathcal B_n\), each layer contains at most \(n+2H+1\) relevant points.
Put \(C_h=|u|+|v|+|v-u|\). Therefore

\begin{equation}\label{eq:long-domain-folner-bound}
\frac{|(\mathcal B_n-h)\triangle\mathcal B_n|}{|\mathcal B_n|}
\le
\frac{C_h(n+2H+1)}{|\mathcal B_n|}
\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}

where \eqref{eq:long-domain-cardinality} and \(L_n=O(\log n)=o(n)\) were
used. Thus \((\mathcal B_n)\) is a Følner sequence in \(\mathbb Z^2\).
The comparison convolution symbol is

\[
F_c(\theta_1,\theta_2)=g_c(\theta_1)g_c(\theta_2),
\]

with a uniform positive lower bound. The square root spectral average in
Corollary~\ref{cor:folner-comparison-transfer} factorizes as

\begin{align}\label{eq:endpoint-factorization}
\alpha_c
&=\frac1{(2\pi)^2}\int\!\!\int\sqrt{F_c} \notag\\
&=\left[\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
\sqrt{g_c(\theta)}\,d\theta\right]^2
=p_1(c).
\end{align}

\emph{Transfer to the full ensemble.}
The kernel \(k(u,v)=c^{|u|+|v|}\) is Hermitian, belongs to
\(\ell^1(\mathbb Z^2)\), and has \(k(0,0)=1\). Equations
\eqref{eq:short-interval-mass}, \eqref{eq:comparison-operator}, and
\eqref{eq:long-domain-folner-bound} verify all remaining hypotheses of
Corollary~\ref{cor:folner-comparison-transfer}. That corollary transfers the
comparison kernel limit simultaneously to the optimum and SRM of the full,
untruncated physical Gram family, giving
\(\alpha_c^2=p_1(c)^2\).
\end{proof}
Figure~\ref{fig:analytic-limits}(b) contrasts the two laws:
$p_1(c^2)$ for uniform translations of a known long interval and
$p_1(c)^2$ for the uniform prior over unknown length intervals.

\subsection{The boundary layer at unit overlap}
\label{a-nonuniform-boundary-at-unit-overlap}

The pointwise fixed-overlap limit in Theorem~\ref{thm:unknown-length} does not
resolve sequences with \(c_n\to1\). At \(c_n=1\), all interval labels
represent the same state, and every equal-prior criterion equals the
exact-label baseline \(1/M_n\). For a moving overlap, write

\begin{equation}\label{eq:moving-physical-gram}
 G_n(c)=\bigl(c^{|I\triangle J|}\bigr)_{I,J\in\mathcal I_n},
 \qquad G_n:=G_n(c_n),
\end{equation}

The amplitude-overlap boundary-layer parameter is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-parameter}
 L_n=\log n,
 \qquad
 \tau_n=n(1-c_n)L_n^2,
\end{equation}

not the squared-overlap scale \(n(1-c_n^2)L_n^2\).  Put

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-amplitude}
 A(t)=1+\frac{2\sqrt t}{\pi}+\frac{\sqrt2\,t}{\pi^2},
 \qquad F(t)=A(t)^2,
\end{equation}

and, for an \(M\)-label Gram matrix, define the trace benchmark

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-trace-benchmark}
 P_{\rm tr}(G)=\left(\frac{\operatorname{tr}\sqrt G}{M}\right)^2.
\end{equation}

Two complementary estimates are required. When \(\tau_n=O(1)\), the vacuum
and one-excitation sectors contribute at leading order, so the proof retains
their additive amplitudes. When \(n p_1(c_n)\to\infty\), the two-endpoint hull
dominates and a relative comparison yields \(p_1(c_n)^2\). Theorem
\ref{thm:unknown-length-ultracritical} treats the first regime, and Theorem
\ref{thm:unknown-length-hull-dominance} treats the second.

We use two normalization lemmas for the orthogonal excitation sectors.

\begin{lemma}[Subnormalized trace bound]\label{lem:subnormalized-trace-bound}
Let \(v_1,\ldots,v_M\) be possibly subnormalized vectors with equal external
weights \(1/M\), let \(X\) be their Gram matrix, and let
\(P_{\rm opt}(v)\) denote their minimum-error objective.  Then

\begin{equation}\label{eq:subnormalized-trace-bound}
 \left(\frac{\operatorname{tr}\sqrt X}{M}\right)^2
 \le \frac1M\sum_{j=1}^M(\sqrt X)_{jj}^2
 \le P_{\rm opt}(v).
\end{equation}

If \(K\succeq|v_j\rangle\langle v_j|\) for every \(j\), then

\begin{equation}\label{eq:subnormalized-dual-normalization}
 MP_{\rm opt}(v)\le\operatorname{tr}K.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The polar, or pretty-good, measurement on the span of the vectors has success
\(M^{-1}\sum_j(\sqrt X)_{jj}^2\); Cauchy--Schwarz gives the first inequality.
The second is feasibility of that measurement.  For the last assertion,
\(K/M\) is feasible for the Bayes dual.  Zero vectors and a singular \(X\)
are allowed throughout.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Orthogonal-sector Minkowski bound]
\label{lem:critical-sector-minkowski}
Suppose \(|\phi_j\rangle=\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^s|\phi_{\ell j}\rangle\), and
let \(P_{\rm opt}^{(\ell)}\) be the equal-prior optimum for the
subnormalized sector vectors \(\{|\phi_{\ell j}\rangle\}_{j=1}^M\).  Then

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-sector-minkowski}
 \sqrt{P_{\rm opt}}
 \le\sum_{\ell=1}^s\sqrt{P_{\rm opt}^{(\ell)}}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a full POVM \(\{M_j\}\), set
\(x_{\ell j}=M^{-1/2}M_j^{1/2}\phi_{\ell j}\).  Minkowski's inequality
in the direct sum over labels gives

\[
 \left(\sum_j\left\|\sum_\ell x_{\ell j}\right\|^2\right)^{1/2}
 \le \sum_\ell\left(\sum_j\|x_{\ell j}\|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Compressing the POVM to the \(\ell\)th physical sector makes the corresponding
term at most \(\sqrt{P_{\rm opt}^{(\ell)}}\).  Optimize the left-hand side.
\end{proof}

The second endpoint requires a boundary-adapted certificate.  Let
\(r_{m,j}=\mathbf 1_{\{j,\ldots,m\}}\in\mathbb R^m\), let \(U_m\) have
rows \(r_{m,j}^{\mathsf T}\), and put
\(C_m=(U_m^{\mathsf T}U_m)^{1/2}\).

\begin{proposition}[Volterra boundary-layer repair]
\label{prop:critical-volterra-repair}
For all sufficiently large \(m\), there is \(K_m\succeq0\) such that, with
\(\Pi_{m,j}=|r_{m,j}\rangle\langle r_{m,j}|\) and
\(d_{m,x}=(K_m)_{xx}-m^{-1}\operatorname{tr}K_m\),

\begin{align}
 K_m&\succeq\Pi_{m,j}\quad(1\le j\le m),
 \label{eq:critical-volterra-domination}\\
 \operatorname{tr}K_m
 &\le\frac{m(\log m)^2}{\pi^2}
 \left[1+C\frac{\log\log m}{\log m}\right],
 \label{eq:critical-volterra-trace}\\
 \sum_{x=1}^m|d_{m,x}|
 &\le \frac{C}{\log m}\operatorname{tr}K_m.
 \label{eq:critical-volterra-flatness}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
The suffix leverage satisfies, uniformly in \(j\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-volterra-leverage}
 \langle r_{m,j},(U_m^{\mathsf T}U_m)^{-1/2}r_{m,j}\rangle
 \le\frac1\pi\left(\log m+\log_+\frac mj\right)+C.
\end{equation}

Set \(\delta_m=3\log\log m/\log m\) and
\(h_m=\lceil m^{1-\delta_m}\rceil\).  The bulk suffixes \(j\ge h_m\) are
dominated by a multiple of \(C_m\).  A cyclic certificate repairs the first
\(h_m-1\) coordinates after splitting each remaining suffix at \(h_m\).
The bounds \(h_m/m=O((\log m)^{-3})\),
\(\operatorname{tr}C_m=m\log m/\pi+O(m)\), and
\eqref{eq:critical-volterra-leverage} give
\eqref{eq:critical-volterra-trace}.  The odd-cosine diagonal estimate for
\(C_m\), together with the lower-order repair trace, gives
\eqref{eq:critical-volterra-flatness}.  The complete construction and its
uniform constants are proved in the Supplemental Material.
\end{proof}

The two endpoints contribute a triangular factor.

\begin{lemma}[Triangular one-half factor]
\label{lem:critical-triangular-half}
Let \(K_m\succeq0\), \(T_m=\operatorname{tr}K_m\), and suppose
\(\sum_x|(K_m)_{xx}-T_m/m|=o(T_m)\).  For

\[
 \Lambda_m=\{(x,y)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}^2:x+y\le m+1\},
\]

one has

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-triangular-half}
 \operatorname{tr}\!\left[
 P_{\Lambda_m}(K_m\otimes K_m)P_{\Lambda_m}\right]
 =\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)T_m^2.
\end{equation}

Under \eqref{eq:critical-volterra-flatness}, the relative remainder is
\(O(1/\log m)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Writing \(k_x=(K_m)_{xx}\) and \(\bar k=T_m/m\),

\[
 \sum_{x,y}|k_xk_y-\bar k^2|
 \le2T_m\sum_x|k_x-\bar k|.
\]

The trace in \eqref{eq:critical-triangular-half} is
\(\sum_{(x,y)\in\Lambda_m}k_xk_y\), and
\(|\Lambda_m|/m^2=1/2+O(m^{-1})\).
\end{proof}

For \(0\le t\le T\), set

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-overlap-family}
 c_n(t)=1-\frac{t}{nL_n^2},
 \qquad
 (G_{n,t})_{I,J}=c_n(t)^{|I\triangle J|}.
\end{equation}

For large enough \(n\), \(c_n(t)>0\) uniformly on every fixed interval
\([0,T]\).  At \(t=0\), \(G_{n,0}=J_{M_n}\) and all conclusions below are
exact.

\begin{theorem}[Ultracritical unknown-length law]
\label{thm:unknown-length-ultracritical}
For every \(T<\infty\), there are \(C_T<\infty\) and \(n_T\) such that,
for \(n\ge n_T\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-uniform-rate}
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}
 \max_X
 \left|M_nP_X(G_{n,t})-F(t)\right|
 \le C_T\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}.
\end{equation}

Here and below, a maximum indexed by \(X\) ranges over
\(\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{SRM},\mathrm{opt}\}\).

Consequently, if \(\tau_n\to\tau\in[0,\infty)\) in
\eqref{eq:critical-parameter}, then

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-common-limit}
 M_nP_X(G_n)\longrightarrow F(\tau)
 \quad\text{for every }X.
\end{equation}

If a finite-size bound is centered at the fixed value \(F(\tau)\), its
right-hand side must additionally contain
\(C_T[\sqrt{|\tau_n-\tau|}+|\tau_n-\tau|]\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
After deleting a finite prefix when necessary, take \(0<c_n\le1\), set
\(s_n=\sqrt{1-c_n^2}\), \(q_n=s_n/c_n\), and expand the physical states in
excitation number.  The state matrix splits into the orthogonal column
sectors \(A_{0,n},A_{1,n},A_{\ge2,n}\).  With \(D_n\) diagonal on interval
labels, \((D_n)_{I,I}=c_n^{|I|}\), one has

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-sector-factorization}
 A_{1,n}=q_nD_nB_{1,n},
 \qquad
 A_{\ge2,n}=q_n^2D_nZ_nW_n,
\end{equation}

where \((B_{1,n})_{I,x}=\mathbf1_{\{x\in I\}}\),
\((Z_n)_{I,(s,t)}=\mathbf1_{\{s<t,\,[s,t]\subseteq I\}}\), and

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-hull-weight}
 (W_n)_{(s,t),(s,t)}=c_n^{-(t-s-1)},
 \qquad I\preceq W_n\preceq c_n^{-(n-2)}I.
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:critical-sector-factorization} is exact: excitation
subsets with the same hull have identical incidence columns, and the sum of
their squared coefficients is
\(q_n^4c_n^{-2(t-s-1)}\).  The singleton labels are zero vectors in the
\(\ge2\) sector, but the external Bayes normalization remains \(M_n\).

The exact one-dimensional singular values and a balanced endpoint
macroblock pinching, proved in the Supplemental Material, give

\begin{align}
 \|B_{1,n}\|_{S_1}
 &=\frac{n^{3/2}}{\pi}L_n+O(n^{3/2}),
 \label{eq:critical-b1-trace}\\
 \|Z_n\|_{S_1}
 &\sim\frac{n^2}{2\pi^2}L_n^2.
 \label{eq:critical-b2-trace}
\end{align}

Uniformly for \(0\le t\le T\), both
\(s_n^2nL_n^2\) and \(q_n^2nL_n^2\) equal
\(2t[1+O_T((nL_n^2)^{-1})]\); the corresponding square-root expansion holds
for \(q_n\sqrt n\,L_n\).  At \(t=0\), these identities hold directly.

Choose \(R_n\asymp L_n/\log L_n\) endpoint macroblocks.  Retaining the
matched off-diagonal two-endpoint cells and centering both row coordinates
makes their row range orthogonal to the endpoint-additive range containing
the vacuum and one-excitation sectors.  Rectangular Schatten--1 pinching then
gives the quantitative global trace lower bound

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-global-trace-lower}
 \frac{\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{G_{n,t}}}{\sqrt{M_n}}
 \ge A(t)-C_T\frac{\log L_n}{L_n}.
\end{equation}

The matched-cell loss is
\(O(R_n^{-1}+\log R_n/L_n+L_n^{-1})\).  The physical length and hull weights
add only \(O_T(L_n^{-2})\), because
\(1-c_n(t)^n\) and \(c_n(t)^{-(n-2)}-1\) have that order uniformly on
\([0,T]\).  The full trace law \eqref{eq:critical-b2-trace} is used separately
for inner matching.

For the upper bound, the vacuum vectors are collinear and
\(M_nP_{\rm opt}^{(0)}=c_n(t)^2\).  A cyclic interval-frame certificate and
Proposition~\ref{prop:critical-volterra-repair}, tensorized and compressed by
Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-triangular-half}, give

\begin{align}
 M_nP_{\rm opt}^{(1)}
 &\le\frac{4t}{\pi^2}\left(1+\frac{C_T}{L_n}\right),
 \label{eq:critical-one-sector-upper}\\
 M_nP_{\rm opt}^{(\ge2)}
 &\le\frac{2t^2}{\pi^4}
 \left(1+C_T\frac{\log L_n}{L_n}\right).
 \label{eq:critical-two-sector-upper}
\end{align}

For \eqref{eq:critical-two-sector-upper}, congruence by \(q_n^2W_n\)
turns the triangular tensor certificate into a dual feasible operator for
the exact physical hull sector.  Lemma~\ref{lem:critical-sector-minkowski}
now yields

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-optimum-upper}
 \sqrt{M_nP_{\rm opt}(G_{n,t})}
 \le A(t)+C_T\frac{\log L_n}{L_n}.
\end{equation}

Finally,
\(P_{\rm tr}\le P_{\rm SRM}\le P_{\rm opt}\), so
\eqref{eq:critical-global-trace-lower} and
\eqref{eq:critical-optimum-upper} squeeze all three square-root amplitudes.
Squaring proves \eqref{eq:critical-uniform-rate}.  Continuity of \(F\), with
\(|F(u)-F(v)|\le C_T(\sqrt{|u-v|}+|u-v|)\), proves the remaining assertions.
\end{proof}

The Supplemental Material proves a stronger local law, uniform on compact
parameter intervals, for the diagonal of \(\sqrt{G_{n,t}}\).  This qualitative
refinement is independent of
the global trace and dual bounds that yield the explicit rate above.

\begin{corollary}[Inner matching to the fixed-overlap law]
\label{cor:critical-inner-matching}
Suppose

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-inner-window}
 \tau_n=n(1-c_n)L_n^2\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad n(1-c_n)=\frac{\tau_n}{L_n^2}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}

Then, for \(X\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{SRM},\mathrm{opt}\}\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-inner-law}
 M_nP_X(G_n)\sim\frac{2\tau_n^2}{\pi^4}\sim A(\tau_n)^2.
\end{equation}

Moreover, this leading term agrees with the endpoint expansion of the
fixed-overlap law:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-inner-p1-matching}
 M_np_1(c_n)^2\sim\frac{2\tau_n^2}{\pi^4}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
Under \eqref{eq:critical-inner-window}, \(c_n^n\to1\) and the upper hull
factor in \eqref{eq:critical-hull-weight} tends to one.  Hence

\[
 c_n^n\|Z_n\|_{S_1}
 \le\|D_nZ_nW_n\|_{S_1}
 \le c_n^{-(n-2)}\|Z_n\|_{S_1}.
\]

Together with \eqref{eq:critical-b2-trace} and
\(q_n^2nL_n^2=2\tau_n[1+o(1)]\), the full physical \(\ge2\)-sector trace
amplitude obeys \(\sqrt2\,\tau_n/\pi^2[1+o(1)]\).  The fixed-\(\delta\)
Volterra tensor dual gives the matching sector optimum after first taking
\(n\to\infty\) and then \(\delta\downarrow0\).  The vacuum and one-excitation
sector amplitudes are respectively \(O(1)\) and \(O(\sqrt{\tau_n})\), hence
lower order.  Sector Minkowski and the trace squeeze prove
\eqref{eq:critical-inner-law}.

Finally, with \(\varepsilon=1-c\downarrow0\),

\[
 p_1(c)^2
 =\frac{4\varepsilon^2}{\pi^4}
 \log^4\!\frac8\varepsilon\,[1+o(1)].
\]

Substitution of \(\varepsilon_n=\tau_n/(nL_n^2)\) proves
\eqref{eq:critical-inner-p1-matching}, because
\(\log(8/\varepsilon_n)/L_n\to1\).  This is a match between two asymptotic
formulas, not an application of the pointwise fixed-\(c\) theorem to a moving
sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hull-dominance criterion]
\label{thm:unknown-length-hull-dominance}
Suppose

\begin{equation}\label{eq:hull-dominance-assumptions}
 \begin{aligned}
 c_n&\to1,\qquad c_n<1\ \text{eventually},\\
 h_n&:=n p_1(c_n)\to\infty.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Then, without a monotonicity assumption and simultaneously for
\(X\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{SRM},\mathrm{opt}\}\), under the uniform prior
\(1/M_n\) on all nonempty intervals,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:hull-dominance-law}
 \frac{P_X(G_n(c_n))}{p_1(c_n)^2}\longrightarrow1.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof overview]
The detailed estimates are given in the Supplemental Material. Here is the
regime closure. Set \(\lambda_n=n(1-c_n)\). From any subsequence, pass to a
further subsequence on which \(\lambda_n\) converges in \([0,\infty]\). If
\(\lambda_n\to0\), the endpoint expansion of \(p_1\), together with
\(h_n\to\infty\), forces \(\tau_n\to\infty\) and reduces the claim to
Corollary~\ref{cor:critical-inner-matching}. If
\(\lambda_n\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\), the weighted continuum estimate in the
Supplemental Material applies uniformly on compact parameter intervals. If
\(\lambda_n\to\infty\),
its adaptive macroblock estimate applies. Each case uses the same physical
Gram matrix \eqref{eq:moving-physical-gram}, the same \(M_n\), and the same
external uniform prior. The Supplemental Material also supplies the
dimension-filling step needed when the extracted subsequence is sparse. Thus
every subsequence has a further subsequence on which
\eqref{eq:hull-dominance-law} holds, proving the full limit.
\end{proof}

The two theorems cover complementary scales. On a fixed compact
\(\tau_n\)-window, the hull parameter \(h_n\) remains bounded and
Theorem~\ref{thm:unknown-length-ultracritical} retains the vacuum and
one-excitation contributions. When \(h_n\to\infty\),
Theorem~\ref{thm:unknown-length-hull-dominance} gives the relative
\(p_1(c_n)^2\) law. For every
compact \(K\Subset(0,\infty)\), the continuum specialization has the stronger
uniform form

\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuum-interface-law}
 \sup_{\lambda\in K}\max_X
 \left|
 \frac{M_nP_X\bigl(G_n(1-\lambda/n)\bigr)}{(\log n)^4}
 -\frac{2\lambda^2}{\pi^4}
 \right|\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}

The hull-dominance criterion also includes the moving-outer regime
\(n(1-c_n)\to\infty\). Conditions
\eqref{eq:hull-dominance-assumptions} are sufficient; whether they are
necessary remains open.

\section{Joint Bayes detection and exact localization}
\label{joint-detection-and-exact-localization}

To include a no-change hypothesis, adjoin \(H_0\), represented by the reference
state

\[
|\Omega\rangle=|0\rangle^{\otimes n}
\]

and assign it fixed prior \(\pi_0\in(0,1)\). An explicit no-change label is
included in the at-most-one- and at-most-two-change formulations of
Ref.~\cite{mohan2023}; the result below concerns a returning interval and a
no-change prior that remains nonzero as \(n\to\infty\).

For known length, the complete joint prior is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:h0-known-joint-prior}
\Pr(H_0)=\pi_0,
\qquad
\Pr(a)=\frac{1-\pi_0}{N},
\quad 1\le a\le N.
\end{equation}

For unknown length, it is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:h0-unknown-joint-prior}
\Pr(H_0)=\pi_0,
\qquad
\Pr(I)=\frac{1-\pi_0}{M_n},
\quad I\in\mathcal I_n.
\end{equation}

Thus the anomaly label is conditionally uniform given that an anomaly is
present. The task is successful only if the measurement reports \(H_0\) for
\(|\Omega\rangle\), or reports the exact anomalous interval otherwise. We
write \(P_{\rm joint}^*\) for the Bayes optimum over all POVMs. For a pure-state
ensemble with priors \(p_j\), its prior-weighted Gram matrix and weighted
square-root (pretty-good) measurement success are

\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-gram-srm}
W_{jk}=\sqrt{p_jp_k}\langle\phi_j|\phi_k\rangle,
\qquad
\mathcal P(W)=\sum_j\left|(\sqrt W)_{jj}\right|^2.
\end{equation}

The priors are already incorporated into \(W\), so no additional factor equal
to the reciprocal number of labels appears in \(\mathcal P(W)\). For equal
priors on \(M\) labels, \(W=G/M\) and
\(\mathcal P(W)=P_{\rm SRM}(G)\). We denote the augmented value by
\(P_{\rm wSRM}^{H_0}\).

\subsection{Weighted SRM decoupling}

\begin{theorem}[No-change decoupling for the weighted SRM]
\label{thm:h0-weighted-srm-decoupling}
Let \(\{|\phi_j\rangle:j\in\mathcal J\}\) be normalized pure states with
conditional anomaly prior \(q_j\), where \(q_j\ge0\) and \(\sum_jq_j=1\).
Let

\[
B_{jk}=\sqrt{q_jq_k}\langle\phi_j|\phi_k\rangle
\]

be their conditional prior-weighted Gram matrix. After adjoining a normalized
state \(|\Omega\rangle\) with prior \(\pi_0\in(0,1)\), put

\begin{align}\label{eq:h0-weighted-blocks}
A&=
\begin{pmatrix}
\pi_0&z^*\\
z&(1-\pi_0)B
\end{pmatrix},
&
D&=[\pi_0]\oplus(1-\pi_0)B,\\
z_j&=\sqrt{\pi_0(1-\pi_0)q_j}\,
\langle\phi_j|\Omega\rangle,
&
\mu&=\sum_jq_j|\langle\Omega|\phi_j\rangle|^2.
\notag
\end{align}

Then

\begin{equation}\label{eq:h0-arrow-trace-norm}
\|A-D\|_1=2\sqrt{\pi_0(1-\pi_0)\mu}
\end{equation}

and

\begin{align}\label{eq:h0-weighted-srm-decoupling}
&\left|
\mathcal P(A)-
\left[\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)\mathcal P(B)\right]
\right| \notag\\
&\qquad\le
2\sqrt2\,[\pi_0(1-\pi_0)\mu]^{1/4}.
\end{align}

Consequently, if \(\mu_n\to0\) and \(\mathcal P(B_n)\to\Lambda\), then

\begin{equation}\label{eq:h0-weighted-srm-affine-transfer}
\mathcal P(A_n)\longrightarrow
\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)\Lambda.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The difference \(A-D\) is the Hermitian arrow matrix

\[
A-D=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&z^*\\ z&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its only nonzero eigenvalues are \(\pm\|z\|_2\), while
\(\|z\|_2^2=\pi_0(1-\pi_0)\mu\). This proves
\eqref{eq:h0-arrow-trace-norm}. Both \(A\) and \(D\) are positive semidefinite
with trace one. Cauchy--Schwarz on the square-root diagonals, followed by the
Powers--St{\o}rmer inequality, gives

\begin{align*}
|\mathcal P(A)-\mathcal P(D)|
&\le
(\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}A}+\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}D})
\|\sqrt A-\sqrt D\|_{\rm HS}\\
&\le2\sqrt{\|A-D\|_1}.
\end{align*}

Finally, positive square roots respect direct sums and nonnegative scalar
multiplication, so

\[
\mathcal P(D)=\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)\mathcal P(B).
\]

Combining these identities proves \eqref{eq:h0-weighted-srm-decoupling} and
the stated limit. No inverse or spectral gap is used.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Known interval length}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed known length with a no-change prior]\label{thm:h0-known-length}
Under the joint prior \eqref{eq:h0-known-joint-prior}, for fixed \(i\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:h0-fixed-limit}
P_{\rm joint}^*(N,i,r,\pi_0)
\longrightarrow
\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)P_\infty(i,r).
\end{equation}

If \(i=i_n\to\infty\) and \(N_n\to\infty\), then

\begin{equation}\label{eq:h0-growing-limit}
P_{\rm joint}^*
\longrightarrow
\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)p_1(c^2).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \(r=1\), every anomalous candidate equals \(|\Omega\rangle\). The \(N+1\)
labels are therefore represented by one physical state, with label priors
\(\pi_0\) and \((1-\pi_0)/N\). Hence, exactly at finite \(N\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:h0-fixed-r-one}
P_{\rm joint}^*(N,i,1,\pi_0)
=\max\!\left\{\pi_0,\frac{1-\pi_0}{N}\right\}
\longrightarrow\pi_0.
\end{equation}

This agrees with \eqref{eq:h0-fixed-limit} because
\(P_\infty(i,1)=0\). Assume henceforth that \(0\le r<1\).
Put \(q=r^i\) and use

\[
|\Phi_a^{(i)}\rangle
=\sqrt q|\Omega\rangle+\sqrt{1-q}|\xi_a^{(i)}\rangle.
\]

Let \(P_G^*\) and \(P_R^*\) be the equal-prior localization optima for the
original and residual ensembles, respectively. Assign \(|\Omega\rangle\) to
\(H_0\), and use an optimal residual measurement on
\(|\Omega\rangle^\perp\). This feasible measurement gives

\[
\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)(1-q)P_R^*
\le P_{\rm joint}^*.
\]

Conversely, the contribution from \(H_0\) is at most \(\pi_0\). Completing the
anomalous sub-POVM can only increase its conditional localization score, so

\[
P_{\rm joint}^*\le\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)P_G^*.
\]

For fixed \(i\), Corollary~\ref{cor:residual-toeplitz} gives
\((1-r^i)P_R^*\to P_\infty(i,r)\), and the two bounds prove
\eqref{eq:h0-fixed-limit}. In the growing length regime,
\(|P_G^*-P_R^*|\le r^{i/2}\) and \(q\to0\); Theorem~\ref{thm:long-known}
then proves \eqref{eq:h0-growing-limit}. At \(r=0\), all \(N+1\) states are
mutually orthogonal and \(P_{\rm joint}^*=1\), also in agreement with both
displayed limits.
\end{proof}

For fixed known \(i\), the mean squared overlap with \(|\Omega\rangle\) is
\(c^{2i}\) and need not vanish. The weighted-SRM conclusion below therefore
concerns the growing-known-length regime.

\begin{corollary}[Growing known length: weighted SRM]
\label{cor:h0-growing-weighted-srm}
Fix \(\pi_0\in(0,1)\) and \(0\le c<1\). Under the joint prior
\eqref{eq:h0-known-joint-prior}, if \(i_n\to\infty\) and \(N_n\to\infty\),
then

\begin{equation}\label{eq:h0-growing-weighted-srm-limit}
\begin{aligned}
P_{\rm wSRM}^{H_0}
&\longrightarrow\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)p_1(c^2),\\
P_{\rm joint}^*-P_{\rm wSRM}^{H_0}
&\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

At finite size,

\begin{align}\label{eq:h0-growing-weighted-srm-bound}
&\bigl|P_{\rm wSRM}^{H_0}
-[\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)P_{\rm SRM}(G_{N,i})]\bigr| \notag\\
&\qquad\le
2\sqrt2[\pi_0(1-\pi_0)c^{2i}]^{1/4}.
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The conditional anomaly weighted Gram is \(B=G_{N,i}/N\), so
\(\mathcal P(B)=P_{\rm SRM}(G_{N,i})\). Every interval state has overlap
\(c^i\) with \(|\Omega\rangle\), and therefore the mean squared reference
overlap in Theorem~\ref{thm:h0-weighted-srm-decoupling} is

\[
\mu=\frac1N\sum_{a=1}^Nc^{2i}=c^{2i}.
\]

This gives \eqref{eq:h0-growing-weighted-srm-bound}; its right hand side tends
to zero for fixed \(c<1\) and \(i_n\to\infty\). Theorem~\ref{thm:long-known}
gives \(P_{\rm SRM}(G_{N_n,i_n})\to p_1(c^2)\). Combining this with
Theorem~\ref{thm:h0-known-length} proves both limits in
\eqref{eq:h0-growing-weighted-srm-limit}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Unknown interval length}

\begin{theorem}[Unknown length with a no-change prior]\label{thm:h0-unknown-length}
Under the joint prior \eqref{eq:h0-unknown-joint-prior},

\begin{equation}\label{eq:h0-unknown-limit}
P_{\rm joint}^*(n,c,\pi_0)
\longrightarrow
\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)p_1(c)^2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume \(0<c<1\) and use the long interval family \(\mathcal B_n\) from Theorem~\ref{thm:unknown-length}. For \(I\in\mathcal B_n\), put

\[
s_I=\langle\Omega|\Phi_I\rangle=c^{|I|}\le c^{L_n}=:\eta_n\to0
\]

and

\[
|\xi_I\rangle
=\frac{|\Phi_I\rangle-s_I|\Omega\rangle}{\sqrt{1-s_I^2}}.
\]

To make the conditioning explicit, define the full family anomalous
localization optimum

\[
P_{G,n}^{\rm all}
:=\max_{\{M_I\}_{I\in\mathcal I_n}}
\frac1{M_n}\sum_{I\in\mathcal I_n}
\langle\Phi_I|M_I|\Phi_I\rangle
=P_{\rm opt}(G^{(n)}),
\]

and define the two equal-prior optima on the retained family by

\begin{align*}
P_{G,n}^{\mathcal B}
&:=\max_{\{M_I\}_{I\in\mathcal B_n}}
\frac1{|\mathcal B_n|}\sum_{I\in\mathcal B_n}
\langle\Phi_I|M_I|\Phi_I\rangle,\\
P_{R,n}^{\mathcal B}
&:=\max_{\{M_I\}_{I\in\mathcal B_n}}
\frac1{|\mathcal B_n|}\sum_{I\in\mathcal B_n}
\langle\xi_I|M_I|\xi_I\rangle.
\end{align*}

In each maximization the operators form a POVM on the support of the
corresponding conditional ensemble, completed arbitrarily on its orthogonal
complement. These are conditional localization probabilities: the factors
\(1-\pi_0\) and \(1-\varepsilon_n\) are not included in their definitions.
The pure state trace distance gives the uniform comparison

\[
|P_{R,n}^{\mathcal B}-P_{G,n}^{\mathcal B}|\le\eta_n.
\]

Use the optimal residual POVM for the long intervals in \(|\Omega\rangle^\perp\), assign zero operators to deleted short intervals, and assign the remaining operator to \(H_0\). With \(\varepsilon_n=1-|\mathcal B_n|/M_n\), this gives

\[
P_{\rm joint}^*
\ge\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)(1-\varepsilon_n)(1-\eta_n^2)
(P_{G,n}^{\mathcal B}-\eta_n).
\]

The universal upper bound is

\[
P_{\rm joint}^*
\le\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)P_{G,n}^{\rm all}.
\]

The retained family analysis in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:unknown-length} gives
\(P_{G,n}^{\mathcal B}\to p_1(c)^2\), whereas
\eqref{eq:unknown-length-limit} gives the full-family limit
\(P_{G,n}^{\rm all}\to p_1(c)^2\). Since
\(\varepsilon_n,\eta_n\to0\), the lower and upper bounds both converge to the
right hand side of \eqref{eq:h0-unknown-limit}. If \(c=0\), all hypotheses are
mutually orthogonal and the success probability is one. If \(c=1\), all
hypotheses are identical and the optimum is
\(\max\{\pi_0,(1-\pi_0)/M_n\}\to\pi_0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unknown length: weighted SRM]
\label{cor:h0-unknown-weighted-srm}
Fix \(\pi_0\in(0,1)\) and \(0\le c<1\). Under the joint prior
\eqref{eq:h0-unknown-joint-prior},

\begin{equation}\label{eq:h0-unknown-weighted-srm-limit}
\begin{aligned}
P_{\rm wSRM}^{H_0}
&\longrightarrow\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)p_1(c)^2,\\
P_{\rm joint}^*-P_{\rm wSRM}^{H_0}
&\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Here \(B=G^{(n)}/M_n\), so
\(\mathcal P(B)=P_{\rm SRM}(G^{(n)})\). The mean squared reference overlap is

\begin{align}
\mu_n(c)
&=\frac1{M_n}\sum_{\ell=1}^n(n-\ell+1)c^{2\ell}
\label{eq:h0-unknown-mean-overlap}\\
&=\frac{2c^2[n-(n+1)c^2+c^{2n+2}]}
{n(n+1)(1-c^2)^2} \notag\\
&\le\frac{2c^2}{(1-c^2)(n+1)}\longrightarrow0.
\notag
\end{align}

The equality follows by differentiating the finite geometric sum; the bound
follows from
\(\sum_{\ell=1}^n(n-\ell+1)c^{2\ell}
\le n\sum_{\ell\ge1}c^{2\ell}\). Theorem~\ref{thm:h0-weighted-srm-decoupling}
therefore gives

\begin{align}\label{eq:h0-unknown-weighted-srm-bound}
&\bigl|P_{\rm wSRM}^{H_0}
-[\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)P_{\rm SRM}(G^{(n)})]\bigr|\notag\\
&\qquad\le
2\sqrt2[\pi_0(1-\pi_0)\mu_n(c)]^{1/4}
=O_{c,\pi_0}(n^{-1/4}).
\end{align}

At \(c=0\) the comparison is exact. For \(0<c<1\), combine
\eqref{eq:h0-unknown-weighted-srm-bound} with
Theorem~\ref{thm:unknown-length}, which gives
\(P_{\rm SRM}(G^{(n)})\to p_1(c)^2\), and with
Theorem~\ref{thm:h0-unknown-length}. The displayed order is only the
no-change decoupling error; it is not asserted as a rate for the full
asymptotic law.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Unit-overlap endpoint and MAP repair}

\begin{proposition}[Indistinguishable endpoint for the weighted SRM]
\label{prop:h0-weighted-srm-unit-overlap}
At \(c=1\), let \(K=N\) for known length and \(K=M_n\) for unknown length.
Then, exactly at finite size,

\begin{equation}\label{eq:h0-weighted-srm-unit-overlap}
\begin{aligned}
P_{\rm wSRM}^{H_0}
&=\pi_0^2+\frac{(1-\pi_0)^2}{K},\\
P_{\rm joint}^*
&=\max\!\left\{\pi_0,\frac{1-\pi_0}{K}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Consequently, as \(K\to\infty\),

\begin{equation}\label{eq:h0-weighted-srm-unit-gap}
\begin{aligned}
P_{\rm wSRM}^{H_0}&\to\pi_0^2,\\
P_{\rm joint}^*-P_{\rm wSRM}^{H_0}
&\to\pi_0(1-\pi_0)>0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
All labels represent the same physical state. If \(p_j\) are their complete
priors and \(u_j=\sqrt{p_j}\), the weighted Gram matrix is \(uu^*\) with
\(\|u\|_2=1\). It is a rank-one projection, so its positive square root is
itself and \(\mathcal P(uu^*)=\sum_jp_j^2\). The Bayes optimum is the largest
label prior. Substituting \(p_0=\pi_0\) and
\(p_j=(1-\pi_0)/K\) proves both formulas.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Weighted SRM with MAP postprocessing]
\label{cor:h0-map-weighted-srm}
After the weighted SRM, classically relabel each measurement outcome by a
maximum-a-posteriori rule. For every fixed \(0\le c\le1\), this postprocessed
measurement is asymptotically Bayes optimal in both the growing-known-length
and unknown-length regimes.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Classical MAP relabeling cannot decrease success, and no postprocessed
measurement can exceed the Bayes optimum. For fixed \(c<1\), the raw weighted
SRM and the optimum already have the same limit by
Corollaries~\ref{cor:h0-growing-weighted-srm} and
\ref{cor:h0-unknown-weighted-srm}, so the claim follows by squeezing. At
\(c=1\), the outcome distribution is independent of the label; for all
sufficiently large \(K\), MAP relabeling reports \(H_0\) and attains success
\(\pi_0=P_{\rm joint}^*\).
\end{proof}

Raw solver objectives are retained as diagnostics but are not used directly as
bounds. Let \(\rho_j\) denote the prior weighted canonical states on their
\(d_{\rm supp}\)-dimensional support. For a numerical dual matrix \(Y\), set

\[
\delta_D=\max_j\max\{0,-\lambda_{\min}(Y-\rho_j)\}
\]

and add the recorded scale-aware roundoff margin \(\zeta_D>0\). Then

\begin{equation}\label{eq:safe-dual-bound}
Y_{\rm safe}=Y+(\delta_D+\zeta_D)I,\qquad
U_{\rm safe}=\operatorname{tr}Y_{\rm safe}
\end{equation}

is rechecked against every dual PSD constraint. For exact matrices satisfying
those constraints, it supplies a feasible upper bound.

For the primal matrices, project each raw \(M_j\) onto the PSD cone and put
\(S=\sum_jM_j^+\). Choose
\(\alpha\geq\max\{1,\lambda_{\max}(S)\}\), with a scale-aware outward margin,
and define

\begin{equation}\label{eq:primal-normalization}
 \begin{aligned}
  Q_j&=\frac{M_j^+}{\alpha},\\
  R&=I-\sum_kQ_k\succeq0,\\
  \overline M_j&=Q_j+\delta_{j1}R.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

This singular-safe construction gives \(\overline M_j\succeq0\) and
\(\sum_j\overline M_j=I\) in exact arithmetic. If \(q\) is the number of
hypotheses, the final scale-aware floor \(f_P>0\), chosen with \(qf_P<1\), is
introduced through the uniform-POVM mixture

\begin{equation}\label{eq:primal-strict-mixture}
 \widehat M_j=(1-qf_P)\overline M_j+f_PI.
\end{equation}

Thus every \(\widehat M_j\) is positive definite and the family remains
complete. The implementation rechecks the resulting POVM. In exact arithmetic,
feasibility and weak duality give

\begin{equation}\label{eq:safe-primal-bound}
L_{\rm safe}=\sum_j\operatorname{tr}(\rho_j\widehat M_j),\qquad
L_{\rm safe}\le P_{\rm opt}\le U_{\rm safe}.
\end{equation}

The implementation, however, uses IEEE double precision and conventional
floating-point eigensolvers, without directed rounding, interval arithmetic,
or exact-rational verification. We therefore report \(L_{\rm safe}\) and
\(U_{\rm safe}\) as safeguarded floating-point bracket endpoints: the displayed
inequality is rigorous conditional on exact feasibility of the postprocessed
matrices, while the stored residuals quantify the numerical recheck at the
working precision.

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:finite-size}(c), the upper endpoints for the SRM
gap remain small over the complete grid. Across the 30 unknown-length cases,
the largest raw absolute primal--dual gap
is \(6.782\times10^{-9}\), while the largest postprocessed floating-point bracket
width \(U_{\rm safe}-L_{\rm safe}\) is \(1.025\times10^{-8}\). The largest
rechecked completeness residual is \(1.41\times10^{-15}\). At the working
precision, no negative eigenvalue was detected in any rechecked primal matrix
or dual slack. The smallest postprocessed primal eigenvalue and dual slack
eigenvalue are respectively
\(3.4104\times10^{-13}\) and \(3.4102\times10^{-13}\). Full dense
SRM computations on the full physical Gram matrix for the single-interval
model extend to \(n=50\), with candidate count \(M_n=1275\), over

\[
c\in\{0.5,0.8,0.9,0.95,0.99\}.
\]

For \(n>7\), only \(P_{\rm SRM}\) is computed. The larger-\(n\) calculations
therefore illustrate finite-size SRM convergence; they neither estimate
\(P_{\rm opt}-P_{\rm SRM}\) nor independently establish SRM optimality.

High-overlap cases converge slowly, as visible in
Figure~\ref{fig:finite-size}(b). The correlation length

\[
\xi(c)=|\log c|^{-1}
\]

is approximately \(99.5\) at \(c=0.99\), so even \(n=50\) has
\(n/\xi\approx0.503\). The corresponding finite-size deviations are
consistent with slow preasymptotic convergence at large overlap.

\section{Conclusion and discussion}
\label{conclusion-and-discussion}
\label{discussion-and-limitations}
\label{conclusion}

We derived exact asymptotic laws for minimum-error localization of one
nonempty returning quantum change interval. For fixed \(i\geq1\), fixed \(c\),
and a prior uniform over the \(N=n-i+1\) translations, the optimum and SRM
converge to \(P_\infty(i,c^2)\) as \(N\to\infty\). If
\(i_n\to\infty\) and \(N_n=n-i_n+1\to\infty\), their common limit is
\(p_1(c^2)\). Under the prior uniform over all nonempty intervals, their common
limit is instead \(p_1(c)^2\). Thus the SRM attains the optimal asymptotic
success probability in all three localization problems.

For moving overlaps with \(\tau_n=n(1-c_n)(\log n)^2\) in a fixed compact
interval, the trace benchmark, SRM, and optimum satisfy
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_nP_X&=
 \left(1+\frac{2\sqrt{\tau_n}}{\pi}
 +\frac{\sqrt2\,\tau_n}{\pi^2}\right)^2\\
 &\quad+O_T\!\left(\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\right),
 \end{aligned}
\]
for \(X\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{SRM},\mathrm{opt}\}\).
The three square-root amplitudes come from the vacuum, one-excitation, and
two-endpoint hull sectors. The complementary hull-dominance theorem states
that, whenever
\[
 c_n\to1,\qquad c_n<1\ \text{eventually},\qquad
 n p_1(c_n)\to\infty,
\]
all three criteria obey \(P_X(G_n(c_n))\sim p_1(c_n)^2\), with no monotonicity
assumption. This sufficient condition unifies the inner, continuum, and
moving-outer regimes. In particular, it gives the continuum law uniformly on
compact parameter intervals for \(c_n=1-\lambda/n\).

The parameters \(c^2\) and \(c\) have distinct geometric origins. A growing
interval of known length has one translation coordinate, and a unit shift
changes two local state factors. An interval of unknown length has two endpoint
coordinates, each contributing a kernel with parameter \(c\); their product
gives \(p_1(c)^2\). The exceptional-sector transfer theorem connects this
triangular F{\o}lner comparison with the non-Toeplitz physical Gram matrix. Its
singular-safe dual certificate does not require a uniform operator-norm bound
on the comparison family.

The same decision framework accommodates joint Bayes detection and exact
localization when a no-change hypothesis has fixed prior
\(\pi_0\in(0,1)\) and the anomalous labels retain their stated conditional
prior. If \(\Lambda\) denotes the corresponding asymptotic conditional
localization limit, the joint optimum tends to
\(\pi_0+(1-\pi_0)\Lambda\). For every fixed \(c<1\), the prior-weighted SRM
attains this limit for growing known and unknown interval lengths. At \(c=1\),
the raw weighted SRM tends to \(\pi_0^2\), whereas the optimum tends to
\(\pi_0\); maximum-a-posteriori relabeling restores the optimal limit.

For fixed \(i\) and \(0<r<1\), the bound
\(P_{\rm opt}-P_{\rm SRM}=O_{i,r}(N^{-1/2})\) controls the measurement gap, not
the individual convergence rates. The fixed-overlap theorems are pointwise in
\(c\); the moving-overlap theorems apply to sequences under their stated
conditions, and the local law for the square-root diagonal is qualitative on
compact parameter intervals.

These results benchmark unrestricted collective POVMs. Local protocols are
known for permanent changes and for the \(i=1\) single-anomaly specialization
\cite{sentis2016,llorens2024}. Whether local or sequential receivers attain
the collective limits for fixed \(i\ge2\), growing known lengths, or the full
uniform interval prior remains open. Multiple intervals, varying states or
overlaps, and restrictions on coherent readout require separate analysis. For
the calibrated pure-state returning-interval model studied here, contiguity
reduces the asymptotic discrimination problem to spectral limits in one and
two dimensions.
